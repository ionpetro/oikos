# Oikos: ethics, evidence and model limits

Research and planning pass, 2026-11-02. Source URLs and relevant passages were checked on the web. This document proposes teaching constraints and lab designs; it does not authorize changes outside Oikos. The companion `ethics-sources.json` reserves E1–E11 for the course registry.

## Teaching position

Teach three different achievements: **calculate within a stated model; evaluate whether its assumptions fit; explain which objective or rights constraint one would adopt**. A correct calculation does not settle the other two. A descriptive claim can be false; a normative claim can be reasoned rather than arbitrary. Classifying a statement is not endorsing it. A recommendation often contains both a prediction and a value judgment. [E1]

Use `status:model` for conditional theoretical claims, `evidence` for bounded empirical findings, `ethics` for evaluative principles, and `fictional` for invented cases or parameters. The label cannot do all the work: each model needs visible assumptions; each finding needs its setting; each ranked policy needs an explicit criterion. GDP, income, willingness to pay, tokens and a simulated payoff are different quantities. None is an automatic measure of a person's worth or a complete social objective. [E1–E4]

## Critique rubric for every lesson and lab

Use **clear / revise / not applicable**, with item IDs and a concrete reason. Do not total the categories into a moral score.

1. **Claim type:** Is the task asking for a definition, conditional prediction, empirical inference or evaluative judgment? Can the learner separate “this model predicts” from “this policy ought to be chosen”? Mixed claims should be decomposed, not forced into a false binary. [E1]
2. **Model contract:** Are agents, feasible choices, objective, units, timing, information, behavior and omitted mechanisms stated? Is the comparison against a specified counterfactual? A result is wrong if its prerequisite is missing, even if its arithmetic is right.
3. **Who counts:** Are costs to nonparticipants, unpaid work, future users, affected groups and unequal starting positions visible where relevant? “Not monetized” must not silently mean zero. Separate an efficiency calculation from distribution and rights. [E3, E4, E11]
4. **Evidence fit:** For a real result, name population, intervention/comparison, outcome and time horizon. Separate evidence of an effect from evidence of the mechanism. Discuss uncertainty or transfer limits without teaching that all evidence is equally weak. [E5, E6, E8, E9]
5. **Incentive mechanism:** What exactly earns the reward, who can respond, and what might change besides the intended effort? Include at least one boundary where a simple incentive works as intended. Do not diagnose a worker's character from a response to a stipulated payoff. [E5, E7]
6. **Unique conditional key:** All four alternatives must be plausible yet only one follows under the question's explicit facts and criterion. A true but differently framed principle is not a wrong answer. Genuine tradeoffs belong in reflection, with multiple supported positions.
7. **Numbers and diagrams:** Independently recompute sign, scale, baseline, denominator and units. Distinguish percentage change from percentage points, average from marginal, stocks from flows, nominal from real, and transfers from resource loss. Name rounding/tolerance. Test boundary values and infeasible regions; never silently clip a model into a different one.
8. **Learning prerequisite:** Teach before scored retrieval, use one-fact contrasts and held-back transfer, and ask where a result stops applying. The cold first guess is unscored. Time pressure is for simple learned retrieval, not multi-step arithmetic, causal evaluation or value tradeoffs.
9. **Revision and reflection:** Can learners state an assumption that would change their conclusion and an observation that would challenge their prediction? Reward completed exploration and reasoning practice; do not attach virtue, ideology or moral worth to an allocation choice.

## Six experiential Rule-lab briefs

All numbers below are invented model parameters, not estimated real effects. Preserve the family flow: **predict before reveal → choose rules/inputs → inspect transparent calculation → change one assumption → reflect**. Calculated outcomes should be reproducible; role reactions must not be fabricated empirical evidence. Label separately what the model computes and what the learner judges.

| Lab | Transparent model and useful boundary | Reflection, without a moral score |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `public-good` | Four players each have 10 tokens. Contribution `c_i` is 0–10; payoff `10-c_i+0.4*sum(c)`. All contribute 10: each gets 16; none: each gets 10. One extra own token costs its donor 0.6 but adds 0.6 to the group total. Predict material-payoff incentives; do not predict that every human must free-ride. Change the multiplier so the private return crosses 1. [E10] | Separate own payoff, group total, communication, repeat interaction and enforcement cost. Who chose the rules, and what preferences did the calculation omit? |
| `metrics` | Compare two stipulated work plans: A completes 10 tasks with 2 errors; B completes 8 with 0. Quantity pay at 2/task gives 20 vs16. Pay 2 per correct task gives16 vs16. Define any rework charge separately; do not label it an objective moral penalty. Add review cost or imperfect error detection. [E5, E7] | Which metric aligns with which goal? Could a quality metric encourage avoiding hard cases? The payoff comparison shows incentives, not a prediction of dishonest behavior. |
| `tax` | Competitive partial-equilibrium toy market: `Qd=100-2*Pc`, `Qs=20+2*Pp`, `Pc-Pp=t`. At t=0: P=20,Q=60; t=10: Pc=25,Pp=15,Q=50; revenue500. With these linear curves and no other distortions, lost surplus is50. Restrict to the interior domain or explicitly compute corner outcomes. Change slopes while holding the baseline comparable. | Legal remittance and economic burden differ. Show buyer/seller burdens and revenue use separately. Distribution or rights is not settled by the surplus total. Economic derivation should also cite the course's core incidence source. |
| `spillover` | `MB=100-Q`, `MC=20+Q`, external marginal damage20. Private crossing givesQ40; aggregate monetized-surplus criterion givesQ30. Stipulated net surplus `60Q-Q²` is800 vs900. A unit tax20 decentralizes the Q30 result under these assumptions; tax receipts are transfers, not another social resource gain. Vary damage or measurement accuracy. [E11] | Who receives benefits, bears harm and controls the evidence? Which important outcomes are not priced? The maximizer of this criterion is not automatically the uniquely fair policy. |
| `commons` | Initial stock100; feasible total harvest H≤100; next stock `min(100,1.2*(100-H))`. H20 leaves96 next period; H40 leaves72. This is an invented regeneration rule, not fishery science. Let users allocate a common harvest cap; expose monitoring costs and never assume compliance merely because a rule is written. [E10] | Compare current access, future stock, rule participation and unequal needs. Change growth or compliance assumptions. Open access and common-property governance are distinct settings. |

Both total120/mean30, but minimum and distribution differ. A transfer is not automatically new production or GDP. [E2–E4] | Compare explicit criteria—equal additions, greatest minimum, need or entitlement—without selecting one as a universal moral key. Add administrative cost only as a stated assumption; do not invent work-response effects. |

## Adaptation risks in the actual Agora family

Read directly: `experiments/agora/app/CONTENT_SCHEMA.md`, `app/src/engine.js`, and `research/family-content-review-ethics.md`. These are implementation evidence, not new authority.

- Keep the existing multi-phase lesson builders, generation, authored minimal contrasts, cold transfer, varied spaced review, retries, hooks and boundary roundups. Do not replace them with a single MCQ per concept or six standalone calculators.
- Preserve Agora's fixes: `qBuild` uses authored wrong tiles; `qSpot` uses authored same-scenario repairs; `qIssue` uses authored issue alternatives or an unscored reviewed scenario. Positive/normative, efficiency/fairness and externality/distribution can overlap; neighboring true statements must never be invented as misconceptions.
- `qNum` currently borrows unrelated numeric answers and manufactures offsets/doubles; this is weak for economics. Prefer authored mistakes tied to the calculation (wrong base, transfer double-counting, percent/points) and ensure they are not aliases or other valid rounded answers. Normalization is not a symbolic algebra engine. Percent, percentage points, money/time units, negatives, zeros, ratios and rounding need explicit regression cases.
- `num` cards are presently eligible for Quick recall by default. Multi-step economic calculations should set `speed:false`; do not allow the fastest response to become evidence of better ethical or economic reasoning.
- `qTermMeaning`/`qTermReverse` already exclude shared accepted names, but semantic overlap still needs human review: price versus cost, profit versus revenue, equality versus equity, and rational versus selfish are not interchangeable labels.
- A binary `grid` is valid only when the facts determine the verdict under a stated criterion. “Should the town adopt the tax?” cannot become a two-stage universal moral key. A valid alternative is “Does Q fall in this specified model, and why?”
- `facts` must point to actual deciding inputs/constraints, not an arbitrary first sentence. `steps` should sequence a genuine calculation or investigation, not pretend that all policy reasoning has one compulsory order.
- Keep source provenance distinct from model authority. Adding `status:model` must not make every fictional parameter a research estimate or every theoretical result an empirical law. Units need visible assumptions; saved progress and XP record practice, never ideology or virtue.
