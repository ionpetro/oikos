# Oikos — economics and incentives

New experience in the Panhellenic / Carpe family, 2 November 2026. Audience: curious English-speaking adults; no calculus or economics background required. Organizing question: what gets rewarded, who bears hidden costs, and how rules shape behavior?

## Orchestration and lineage

The actual Carpe seven-phase recipe is the workflow: parallel source research → synthesized curriculum → copied family core/schema/fixture → native unit authoring → two independent adversarial reviews per unit and correction → browser QA and final independent critic → build and publication. The original model names and historical budgets are context, not execution requirements. Source UI lineage is Carpe → Objection → corrected Agora → Oikos. No existing course is edited.

## Teaching contract

Each of 12 units has four native semantic lessons and a mixed roundup. Target 6–8 carefully written teaching items per lesson; correctness and scope govern length. Include a taught principle with worked examples and two genuinely different checks; useful concept recall; application under explicit assumptions; a misconception or discrimination task; and a held-back transfer linked to a principle earlier in the same lesson. Mix all nine inherited kinds across the course. The fourth lesson of every unit must contain a substantive boundary case for the roundup.

Keep the inherited first unscored guess, direct teaching, immediate retrieval, separate harder phase, genuinely new transfer, older due review, retries, and 1/3/7/14/30/60-day Leitner intervals. Retain forgiving streaks, XP, mascot moods, matching, construction, classification, typed recall, deciding facts, and decision/reason questions where valid. Calculations and value judgments are untimed. Authored numeric distractors must diagnose plausible mistakes; specify units and rounding, with meaningful accepted aliases.

## Economic and ethical correctness

Score definitions, calculations, evidence distinctions, and conditional inference. Normative choices with competing defensible values belong in reflection. A larger monetary sum does not establish a fair or permissible policy. Unpriced is not zero; a transfer is not a new resource; averages can conceal differences. Distinguish a conditional model from empirical evidence, a mechanism hypothesis from an estimated effect, and an efficiency criterion from distribution or rights.

Claim labels: model, evidence, practice, ethics, fictional. Every item has source IDs and concise support/limits available in the UI. Real findings need bounded setting and uncertainty. Original fictional cases and numbers avoid reproducing textbook questions. Scenarios must state decisive conditions; distractors must be plausible yet actually wrong under those conditions. Vary correct positions and natural answer length. Never encode ideology or moral worth as an XP score.

## Six rule labs

The parent's models.js implements six optional experiments inside the inherited lesson shell: metrics, tax, spillover, commons, public-good, and budget. Flow: prediction → select assumptions/rules → inspect baseline and chosen outcomes → show calculation → change one assumption → reflect. Parameters and consequences are fictional. A computed material payoff is not a forecast of human choice. No moral score or policy approval is generated. Inputs are bounded, every combination is checked, arithmetic is independently reviewed, and reflections save locally.

## Review responsibilities

Foundations writer: u01–u06. Institutions writer: u07–u12. Each writer independently adversarially checks the other's six units. The ethics/evidence reviewer independently checks all twelve units, numerical assumptions, and model outputs. The parent resolves integration and UI findings, then an agent gives a final independent critic pass. Review artifacts must record exact item IDs, changes, rejected findings with reasons, and remaining verification limits. Passing schema checks is not proof of educational efficacy.
